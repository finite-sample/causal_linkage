# Permutation bias, heterogeneous effects, and identification: novelty audit

Research date: 8 October 2026. Scope: the exact propositions in `manuscript/paper.tex`, not just the broad claim that linkage matters. Verdict for this subset: **do not claim a new causal-linkage method on the strength of these results.** The arbitrary-fixed-permutation result is a useful, apparently unlocated design-based lemma, but its important interpretation combines explicit antecedents. The observational example is a special case of existing bias formulas; the nonidentification example is an elementary symmetry construction. This is a judgment about substantive contribution, not a claim that every equation was previously printed.

## Does the finite-population permutation result establish a substantive new contribution?

### Takeaway
The factor `(N q - 1)/(N - 1)` is already exactly present in established exchangeable-linkage algebra. The repository changes the probability experiment and permits arbitrary fixed permutation and heterogeneous effects; that is a real distinction, but the reliability–effect covariance insight also has an explicit causal-linkage antecedent. The combination is best presented as a design-based clarification, not an independently sufficient paper contribution.

### Cited Findings
- Chambers (2009), *Regression Analysis of Probability-Linked Data*, printed pp. 9–10, equations (1)–(6), models a complete one-to-one linkage as a random permutation matrix and gives `E(A|X)=(lambda-gamma)I+gamma 11'`, with `lambda+(N-1)gamma=1`. Printed p. 12, equation (11), derives naive OLS bias using this mean matrix; equation (12) corrects it. He explicitly attributes the exchangeable model to Neter et al. (1965). Full primary report downloaded and equations inspected. [Chambers primary report](https://statsnz.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p20045coll4/id/288/download).
- Neter, Maynes and Ramanathan (1965), *The Effect of Mismatching on the Measurement of Response Errors*, JASA 60:1005–1027, develops matching-error models and their effect on response-error relationships. Its publisher abstract was accessible, but its full equations were not directly inspected; the historical model attribution above rests on Chambers's explicit statement, not an asserted direct reading of Neter. [Publisher](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01621459.1965.10480846).
- Tahamont et al. (2021; online 2020), author preproof Appendix E, appendix printed p. 7 (PDF final page), titled “Treatment Heterogeneity Correlated With Matching Error,” derives a subgroup-weighted effect `sum_g P(g)(TPR_g-FPR_g) tau_g`. It allows linkage reliability and treatment effects both to vary by group and explains how opposing subgroup effects permit amplification. This is a direct antecedent to reliability–effect covariance, in a binary outcome ascertainment model rather than permutation linkage. [Author full preproof, Appendix E](https://iamshiyan.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/tahamont2020_preproof.pdf).
- *Differentially Private Linear Regression With Linked Data* (2024), §2.1, equations (2.1)–(2.4), reproduce the doubly stochastic matching-probability formulation, Lahiri–Larsen correction, and ELE model. This forward citation confirms that the exact finite denominator remains standard in current linked-regression work. [Primary article](https://hdsr.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/4if53bjq/release/2).
- Slawski (2025), §2.1–2.2, Proposition 1, treats the three X/treatment/Y layouts and quantifies mismatch bias. Its conditional independence assumptions and iid donor formulation differ from the repository's fixed-population complete-randomization experiment; it is not an exact antecedent of the arbitrary fixed-permutation lemma. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14492v1).

### Inferences
The following are my algebraic comparisons, not formulas claimed to occur verbatim in a source.

**Exact scalar reduction.** Chambers's mean matrix is `Q=kappa I+gamma 11'`, with `kappa=lambda-(1-lambda)/(N-1)=(N lambda-1)/(N-1)`. For any fixed zero-sum contrast `w`, `E_A(w'Ay | y,w)=w'Qy=kappa w'y`. Thus the finite correction itself is an immediate corollary of the established model, not a newly discovered correction to simple attenuation. Taking assignment expectation subsequently gives `kappa tau_N` if its linkage assumptions continue to hold.

**Do not confuse the experiments.** Chambers averages random linkage for fixed analysis data. The repository averages assignment for a fixed arbitrary permutation. A deterministic permutation need not have exchangeable off-diagonal probabilities, so Chambers does not literally subsume the repository theorem. The latter additionally permits correct identities to be concentrated among units with unusual effects. Its concise general form for any fixed matrix `A` is

`E_Z[w(Z)' A Y(Z)] = {N sum_i A_ii tau_i - sum_j (sum_i A_ij) tau_j}/{N(N-1)}`.

This follows by multiplying the standard complete-randomization cross-moments `E(w_i Z_j)=1/N` on the diagonal and `-1/[N(N-1)]` off the diagonal; baseline terms vanish since `E w_i=0`. For a permutation, each column sum is one, yielding the manuscript formula. This derivation clarifies that no delicate new probability argument is needed beyond the known linkage-matrix representation and the randomization second moments. It also explains the absence of the treatment allocation fraction from the expectation.

**Covariance reduction.** Let `r_g=TPR_g-FPR_g` in Tahamont Appendix E. Its displayed subgroup sum equals `E(r) E(tau)+Cov(r,tau)`. The repository adds the exact finite correction under its different sampling experiment: `[(Nq-1)/(N-1)] tau_N + [N/(N-1)] Cov_N(F,tau)`. The substantive lesson that average linkage quality cannot determine bias when reliability and causal effects co-vary is therefore already explicit in causal-linkage literature, not merely inferred from general selection-bias knowledge.

**What survives.** A carefully stated design-based extension: for arbitrary fixed one-to-one errors independent of assignment, only diagonal correctness and its alignment with individual effects matter for the assignment expectation; detailed cycles do not. This is sharper in scope than ELE and is useful pedagogically. I did not locate its exact statement. Its short derivation and absence of a new usable identification/inference consequence make it a modest lemma, not a strong standalone methodological contribution. A fixed derangement's `-tau_N/(N-1)` expectation is likewise an immediate endpoint of the lemma. Do not describe this as disproving attenuation results derived under other linkage models.

### Gaps
- Neter 1965 and Lahiri–Larsen 2005 full original texts were not directly inspected; no theorem-number claim about their exact coverage is justified. Chambers 2009 and the 2024 forward article provide directly inspected equations.
- I did not find the exact arbitrary-permutation heterogeneous-effect theorem in a prior source. This is **not** a priority certificate.
- A new contribution would need a meaningful consequence of that extension: e.g. an identified estimable correction under weaker information, sharp design-specific bounds, a new optimal review design, or uniform causal inference exploiting graph restrictions. None is supplied by the expectation lemma alone.

## Are the within-arm invariance, precision warning, and covariate-confounding results novel?

### Takeaway
No substantive novelty claim is supportable for these results. There are unusually close antecedents, including an explicit within-treatment swap example and a general binary-confounder IPW bias analysis containing the repository's numerical example.

### Cited Findings
- Wortman and Reiter (2018), §3.1, printed pp. 6–7, explicitly swap two treated units' outcomes in a propensity-score subclass and state the difference-in-means estimate is unchanged; they immediately warn that regression adjustment generally changes unless the covariates coincide. §3.2, pp. 7–8, covers treatment-crossing errors and possible sign reversal. Thus harmless identity errors versus harmful errors, and the adjustment qualification, are exact antecedents. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.03631).
- Nab, Groenwold, van Smeden and Keogh (2020), *Quantitative Bias Analysis for a Misclassified Confounder*, Epidemiology 31:796–805, Figure 1B and equation (4), derive IPW ATE bias with a nondifferentially misclassified binary confounder, binary treatment and continuous additive outcome. Their method assumes correct weights based on the observed confounder, precisely addressing why correct measured-data propensity modeling does not remove the resulting confounding. Full text XML inspected; displayed equations are raster images, so their exact typesetting was not transcribed. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7523582/) and [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000001239).
- Slawski (2025), Proposition 1(ii), independently gives bias from incorrectly linked covariates when treatment and outcome are jointly observed. This confirms direct linkage-specific coverage, although the manuscript example uses the propensity conditional on the corrupted covariate rather than plugging the original propensity formula into mismatched X. [Primary preprint, Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14492v1).

### Inferences
**Exact model mapping for the repository's Proposition 8.** In Nab et al.'s notation, take true confounder `L=X`, error-prone confounder `L*=W`, prevalence `P(L=1)=1/2`, treatment probabilities `P(A=1|L=0)=.2` and `P(A=1|L=1)=.8`, and outcome `Y=A+L`. The copy-or-independent-donor mechanism gives sensitivity and specificity both `(1+q)/2`. It is nondifferential conditional on L. Thus the entire data-generating law belongs to the earlier model, not just a vaguely similar measurement-error setup.

An independent reduction yields `P(X=1|W=w)=.5+q(w-.5)`, `Var(X|W)=(1-q^2)/4`, and `Cov(X,Z|W)=.6 Var(X|W)`. Dividing by `e_W(1-e_W)=(1-.36q^2)/4` gives the manuscript's bias `.6(1-q^2)/(1-.36q^2)`. This is a parameter substitution and elementary binary regression identity within an established model. Fitting both nuisance functions correctly for W targets the W-adjusted statistical functional, which is not the causal ATE; ordinary double robustness does not change this target. Calling W “fuzzy linked” does not create a different statistical problem when the mechanism is exactly nondifferential binary misclassification.

The pre-assignment selection formula `tau_S-tau_N=Cov_N(S,tau)/mean(S)` is likewise an algebraic re-expression of a selected mean. It usefully separates target changes from identity errors, but the covariance notation does not itself support originality.

### Gaps
- The repository's donor-with-replacement example is not a full one-to-one covariate permutation model. A proof for the latter with estimated nuisance functions would require additional work; the current result should not be advertised as solving it.
- The broad claim “precision alone is insufficient” is decisively established, but the most useful linkage quality summaries for a particular estimator and graph remain design dependent. A new estimator-specific diagnostic would need separate development and comparison.

## Does the two-unit nonidentification proof or another residual point sustain a novelty claim?

### Takeaway
The two-unit construction is valid under its stated unordered-outcome observation model and usefully avoids confusing one realized ambiguity with observational-law nonidentification. It is nevertheless an elementary label-symmetry example, with no demonstrated new identification frontier. Retain it as exposition, not as a headline discovery.

### Cited Findings
- Balabdaoui, Slawski and Steffani (2025), *Identifiability in Unlinked Linear Regression*, study loss of identification when predictor–response correspondence is absent. §4.1 Theorem 3 and §4.2 Theorem 4 discuss sign and rotational ambiguities induced by symmetry. Their models are not the repository's randomized finite-population experiment, so these are conceptual antecedents rather than a literal theorem containing the N=2 example. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14986v1).
- Slawski (2025), §2.1 discussion following A1, explicitly discusses failure of causal identification when mismatch depends on potential outcomes beyond observed X. This is not the repository's randomized unlabeled-file counterexample but refutes a broad claim that linkage-induced causal nonidentification was previously unnoticed. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14492v1).

### Inferences
With balanced complete randomization, homogeneous potential outcomes `(Y(0),Y(1))=(0,1)` and `(1,0)` generate the same unordered multiset: half zeros and half ones, independent of the identity labels in the assignment roster. The manuscript's N=2 case therefore extends immediately to any even N with N/2 treated. The inability to tell the sign is precisely a treatment-label symmetry of the observation experiment. This mathematical extension is my own comparison, not a claim that the cited ULR theorem already states it.

The balanced-count qualification matters. With unequal known treatment counts, the two homogeneous populations generate different numbers of ones and zeros; that particular pair no longer proves nonidentification. The existence of the N=2 counterexample defeats a universal point-identification claim, but does not characterize identification for general assignments, graph constraints or effect restrictions. A result describing that frontier would be substantially stronger and might deserve a separate novelty search. None should be claimed from the present example.

**Research decision for this assigned subset:** stop treating the expectation, invariance, binary-confounder and two-unit nonidentification results as the source of a novel methods paper. Keep them as a rigorous explanatory compendium, and narrow any further research to a stronger theorem or actionable remedy that changes what practitioners can validly infer. “We derive a finite-population extension for fixed one-to-one linkage errors” is defensible descriptive language; “we discover that linkage precision is insufficient for causal inference” is not.

### Gaps
No exact publication of the N=2 observational-law example was located. Search did not establish absence across the entire statistical matching, ecological inference, shuffled regression and causal data-fusion literatures. This does not affect the assessment that the current proof is elementary and not by itself an adequate contribution.
