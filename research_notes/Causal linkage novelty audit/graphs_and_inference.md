# Feasible identity graphs, causal inference, and review priorities: novelty audit

## Are the graph ranges themselves a new causal method?

### Takeaway
No strong standalone novelty claim survives for the implemented linear-statistic bounds. Candidate graphs supply real additional information beyond an error rate, but using this information in an assignment objective is an established construction. No located source establishes the entire exact package of outside options, graph omissions, erroneous trusted links, and causal weighting; that absence does not make the combination a substantial theoretical advance.

### Cited Findings
- Turkcapar and Krishnan (2023), sections 3.1–3.3, formulate minimum/maximum weighted assignments on candidate graphs, explain donor coupling, permit degree caps including one-to-one matching, and state Proposition 3.1 for a general edge weighting. Candidate containment is the premise for the range. Their printed minimum and maximum formulations differ in inequality direction; the claims that inequalities saturate need sign/cardinality qualifications. This is a close conceptual and optimization precedent, not a warrant to copy every printed formula. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05178v1).
- Sismanis, Wang, Fuxman, Haas, and Reinwald (ICDE 2009) already replace aggregate query answers with upper/lower bounds over unresolved identities without requiring probability distributions. [IBM primary publication page](https://research.ibm.com/publications/resolution-aware-query-answering-for-business-intelligence).
- The current code imposes one assignment-or-outside option per source, donor exclusivity, cardinality bounds, an off-graph edge budget, and a trusted-link violation budget. It maximizes/minimizes a linear objective with binary programming. [Code](../../R/graph.R); [manuscript, section Feasible reconstructions and causal uncertainty](../../manuscript/paper.tex).
- Imai and Yamamoto (2010) already study causal treatment effects under differential treatment misclassification, derive sharp nonparametric identification bounds, and develop sensitivity restrictions. Their Assumption 1 maintains true-treatment ignorability; Proposition 1 characterizes bounds under ignorability plus a restriction on misclassification. There is no individual identity graph in that construction. [Author PDF, pp. 547–551](https://imai.fas.harvard.edu/research/files/merror.pdf).

### Inferences
The exact reduction is simple. For fixed assignment Z and fixed arm counts m,c, let w_i=Z_i/m−(1−Z_i)/c and set edge weight W(i,j)=w_i y_j. A causal difference-in-means statistic is then precisely a weighted matching sum. An OLS coefficient with fixed design uses the corresponding row of (X'X)^−1 X' as w. The fact that these weights have a causal interpretation adds no new optimization result. Signed weights do require correct equality/cardinality constraints; neither selecting every positive-weight edge nor omitting every negative-weight edge is generally admissible.

An outside option is a private dummy donor with endpoint value chosen by the sign of w. Known overlap becomes a cardinality restriction. Missing-edge and false-trusted-link budgets are linear side constraints. General side constraints can destroy network integrality, so binary programming is sensible. This is a coherent useful specification, but proving that its extrema equal the extrema of the set it encodes is a representation argument. It does not establish sharp identification of the ATE, whose missing potential outcomes are a separate source of uncertainty.

The treated-roster case is also an algebraic reduction: if m roster records inject into a complete outcome population of size N, and everyone outside their image is control, the contrast is N/[m(N−m)] times the sum of outcomes in the roster image minus the fixed total divided by N−m. This is a weighted subset-assignment query. Changing treatment/covariates inside an adjusted estimator is harder because its weights change with the reconstruction. The repository explicitly excludes that harder case from its fixed-weight theorem.

A substantive extension would characterize which graph information identifies a causal functional or improves a uniformly valid procedure when identities, treatments, and analysis weights move together. The present linear-range proposition does not do that. Identifying the realized statistic for a given assignment is weaker than identifying the finite-population ATE from an observable probability law.

### Gaps
- I found no primary paper with the exact joint error-budget specification implemented here. The separate constraints are routine integer-programming devices; priority of their conjunction is unresolved and is insufficient by itself for a methods-paper claim.
- Turkcapar–Krishnan's general weighting language is broader than the assumptions that justify some of their printed saturation arguments. The correct comparison is to their weighted-assignment framework plus standard matching theory, not a claim that their literal equations prove every case in this repository.
- No scalable optimizer for an estimator whose treatment/covariate-dependent weights must be recomputed at each reconstruction is implemented.

## Does the causal inference step add a nontrivial new result?

### Takeaway
The max-p and union-of-intervals guarantees are established nuisance-projection logic. A newly located close comparator, Heng and Shaw (2022 preprint, JCGS 2025), already gives finite-population causal sensitivity to outcome errors via integer programming. Feasible identity graphs narrow its uncertainty model, but the current code does not supply the graph-specific computational or inferential advance that could turn that specialization into a strong contribution.

### Cited Findings
- Berger and Boos (1994) maximize a nuisance-indexed p-value over a confidence set, then add the confidence-set failure probability. The confidence set is data-dependent; independence is not required. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476836); [author manuscript scan](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e078b805964bd72ee439f8e5fc50c9cda009a31c&repid=rep1&type=pdf).
- Heng and Shaw, Definition 1, define warning accuracy as the greatest outcome agreement compatible with reversing a randomization-test decision. Section 4.1 formulates it as an integer quadratically constrained problem and expressly permits additional constraints encoding prior information. Their efficient implementations use design-specific symmetry reductions and chi-square/finite-population CLT approximations for Mantel–Haenszel and Neyman tests; the general sensitivity definition is not limited to those approximations. Section 3 also introduces sensitive sets and sensitivity weights to guide validation. [Primary arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.03111); [published JCGS article](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2025.2461222).
- Morucci, Noor-E-Alam, and Rudin (2022) optimize causal test statistics over acceptable treated-control pairings. These are pairs of different units; their uncertainty object is not the correspondence of two records for one unit. Assumptions 1–3 and section 3 make the distinction clear. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02227); [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijds.2022.0020).
- The repository's `graph_null_p` enumerates every full feasible linkage, computes the complete-assignment sharp-null p-value for each, and returns the maximum. It has no outside options or graph/trusted-link budget parameters. `causal_bounds` expands a linear-statistic range by a Hoeffding radius; this is a conservative projection, not an exact sharp causal confidence region. [Code](../../R/graph.R).

### Inferences
**Exact Heng–Shaw reduction.** For binary donor outcomes y, define

F_G = {v in {0,1}^N : v_i = sum_j x_ij y_j, x is a feasible identity matching}.

Their outcome-error feasible set can be intersected with F_G by introducing the same binary x variables and linear equations. If an accepted linked vector is y_tilde, a Hamming budget adds sum_i |v_i−y_tilde_i|≤b, which is linear because y_tilde is fixed and binary. A full permutation implies sum_i v_i=sum_j y_j; closed graph components imply component-specific conservation. Hall restrictions can constrain feasible subsets further. These are actual information restrictions that an overall error rate does not encode.

A small witness makes the distinction concrete. Take Z=(1,1,0,0), donor outcomes (0,1,0,1), and candidate edges allowing arbitrary permutations within donors {1,2} for sources {1,2}, and within donors {3,4} for sources {3,4}. Every feasible linkage gives treatment contrast zero. A generic Hamming budget of two includes (1,1,0,0), giving contrast one, even though it preserves the global number of ones. Thus graph geometry can eliminate substantively important error patterns. This is an original explanatory derivation here, not a priority claim.

An identity-error budget is not the same as a binary-outcome error budget: an incorrect donor can have the same outcome. Identity mismatches upper-bound outcome disagreements, and exclusivity constrains which disagreements occur together. The error metrics must not be equated in comparisons.

**What transfers and what does not.** The warning-accuracy optimization can directly accept identity-feasibility constraints. Its efficient aggregation by observed 2x2 tables need not survive because two records in the same observed cell can have different donor neighborhoods. Deriving efficient exact graph-aware testing, particularly with multiple fields or missing outcomes, is therefore a plausible nontrivial next problem. Merely stating the constrained optimization or enumerating it is not that contribution.

For a full bijection under a sharp null, every reconstruction has the same outcome multiset. Consequently the complete-randomization distribution of the unstudentized difference in means is the same across reconstructions; only the observed contrast changes. For a two-sided absolute statistic, max p corresponds to the feasible observed contrast closest to zero, but finding that attainable point is not in general the same as computing endpoints: feasible values can have gaps. Minimizing absolute contrast itself has a standard mixed-integer linear formulation with an epigraph variable. For binary outcomes the common randomization distribution is hypergeometric. Thus the full-permutation/complete-randomization special case is not a persuasive prospective algorithmic gap either. A harder problem must involve changing donor subsets, blocked designs whose within-block outcome multisets change, nonlinear statistics, or another feature preventing this reduction. None is currently established as a novel result here.

The repository's containment result is precisely the event inclusion
{causal failure} subset {truth omitted from reconstruction set} union {oracle failure}.
It gives alpha+delta by the union bound. Add delta to max-p (capped at one), or use oracle level alpha−delta, to obtain total level alpha. Allowing graph construction to depend on treatment/outcome is therefore not a new exemption from selective inference; validity rests on unconditional oracle validity and unconditional containment. A frozen outcome-selected sample with a fictional assignment law violates the oracle premise.

### Gaps
- No exact same-identity graph causal test paper was found in the searches below. That is a bounded search finding, not proof of first priority.
- The hard premise is obtaining a useful simultaneous containment probability for the entire true linkage, especially with missing candidates. Individual pair calibration or 95% edge coverage does not automatically give 95% whole-graph containment. The implementation takes budgets/containment as assumptions; it does not estimate this guarantee from a validation design.
- Strongest prospective claim: a computationally scalable, design-valid causal sensitivity procedure exploiting identity-graph conservation, with audited uncertainty in candidate recall and useful power/width relative to unconstrained misclassification sensitivity. This remains future work.

## Are global review priorities novel, and what would settle the remaining claim?

### Takeaway
The zero-local-outcome-range example is useful and correct, but downstream-query-aware review and nonlocal information value are old ideas. The repository supplies an illustrative one-step minimax calculation, not a new theory or proven optimal review design.

### Cited Findings
- Jeffery, Franklin, and Halevy (SIGMOD 2008) rank candidate matches for user confirmation by value of perfect information using query-result quality as utility. This is a direct precedent for reviewing identities according to downstream consequences instead of linkage confidence alone. [Author publication page](https://research.google/pubs/pay-as-you-go-user-feedback-for-dataspace-systems/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1376616.1376701).
- Lam et al. (2 August 2026) describe design-based clerical-review sampling stratified by score, comparison pattern, demographic group, and record ambiguity. Their abstract targets match-rate precision and representation, not a causal contrast or guaranteed graph-interval reduction. Abstract-level screening only; this is a relevant review-design comparator, not evidence of exact subsumption. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01401).
- `review_priority` recomputes interval width under each possible identity answer, then reports best-case and guaranteed one-step width reductions. It neither assigns answer probabilities nor optimizes a multi-record review policy; it assumes the truth is among the candidate edges and does not allow “none of these” answers. [Code](../../R/graph.R).
- A recent lead, RCShift (arXiv 2609.26207v1, available before 8 October 2026), concerns which joint contingency-table counters to retain under fixed margins and a declared likelihood-ratio family. Its observation model uses i.i.d. records and table counts; it is explicitly different from identity recovery. It discusses complementarity and task-dependent information, but does not supply causal inference over uncertain person-level identity matchings. [Primary HTML, Observation model and Related Work](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26207v1). Its page displays inconsistent month cues (identifier September; manuscript date August), so use the identifier/version, not search-engine publication age.

### Inferences
The user's subtle point survives as an information insight: a record with identical outcomes across its own candidates can matter greatly because learning its donor changes what other records can use. But any graph-global query optimizer can exhibit that effect, and a three-record example alone is unlikely to support a methods paper. The probability-free best/worst review criterion differs from expected VPI, without automatically constituting a substantial new decision-theoretic method.

**Falsifiable next test.** Fix binary outcomes and a known complete or blocked assignment mechanism. Implement (a) Heng–Shaw-style Hamming sensitivity, (b) that same optimization augmented with identity assignment constraints, and (c) a specialized graph-aware algorithm. Hold candidate recall, accepted-link precision, sample size, and treatment allocation fixed. Require: exact agreement with exhaustive small-case tests; demonstrably tighter uncertainty or higher power because of donor conservation; a computational result beyond simply feeding the added constraints to an integer solver; and calibrated coverage under graph omissions. If (b) already matches (c) at application scale, the proposed methodological novelty largely disappears, though an applied audit/software contribution may remain.

### Gaps
- Full text of Jeffery et al. could not be downloaded from the indexed Buffalo mirror (404); the author page and indexed paper abstract establish the VPI/query-quality precedent, but no claim is made that it contains this exact minimax criterion.
- Broad search also located Sheng, *Treated Records* (July 2026), concerning policy-induced changes to administrative identifiers/categories. Its abstract does not establish a candidate-graph causal method; it should not be used as an exact-method comparator without full-text verification. [Primary SSRN page](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7059940).
- Independent reviewers might value a careful unified applied treatment, but the current graph/inference work does not establish a strong original statistical-method claim. Appropriate status: useful synthesis and implementation; narrow prospective contribution requiring additional results.
