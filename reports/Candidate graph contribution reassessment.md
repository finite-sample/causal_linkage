# Candidate graphs sharpen causal sensitivity analysis

**Yes: the current work supports a defensible, modest contribution as a causal specialization of established methods.** Its strongest result is an executable answer to a specific question: can a randomized study reject the sharp no-effect null under every identity reconstruction permitted by its candidate links and identity-error budget? The procedure preserves one-to-one ownership, computes the worst-case p-value, and returns a linkage attaining it. Targeted research did not locate this exact construction in the closest primary sources. Its mathematical ingredients and the general observation that accuracy misses consequential structure are established. That distinction supports continuing a focused methodological note; it does not support the earlier blanket recommendation to stop. Practical usefulness still needs evidence beyond engineered eight-unit examples.

Audit date: 8 October 2026. This reassessment concerns the implemented [candidate-graph analysis](../research/graph-information.md), with earlier results retained only as background.

## The contribution is a causal decision procedure

Let the treatment assignment be known and completely randomized, and suppose the donor file contains exactly one outcome for every experimental unit. A feasible linkage must respect candidate edges, use every donor once, and obey a stated upper bound on mistaken accepted identities. The method asks whether **any permitted ownership arrangement defeats the causal conclusion**, without selecting a single linkage or specifying probabilities over linkages. This is a concrete input/output procedure, rather than merely a suggestion to retain more linkage metadata. Its witness tells the researcher exactly which ownership arrangement makes the result least convincing. ([Implemented formalization, Sections 1 and 4](../research/graph-information.md))

Under Fisher's sharp null, every full bijection preserves the donor outcome multiset. Complete randomization therefore gives every reconstruction the same reference distribution for the absolute difference in means. If its tail function is \(H\), the robust p-value is

\[
p_{\max}=H\!\left(\min_{\pi\in\mathcal M}|T(\pi)|\right).
\]

A matching-based integer program obtains the smallest attainable absolute contrast. This matters because an interval spanning zero need not contain an attainable zero contrast. Binary outcomes have an exact hypergeometric reference law; general outcomes currently require enumeration of treatment assignments. The optimization avoids enumerating all linkages, but does not establish universal computational scalability. ([Proof and implementation](../research/graph-information.md))

The earlier audit already identified this common-law reduction. The new work makes it a proved, tested procedure with structural diagnostics; the reassessment also corrects an overly broad originality standard. It does not depend on treating a previously noted reduction as a newly discovered theorem.

**Identity error is not outcome error.** Two donors can carry the same outcome, so exchanging their identities consumes an identity-error budget without changing the outcome vector. Conversely, candidate restrictions and donor exclusivity prohibit corrected outcome patterns that satisfy a generic outcome-error budget. The relevant addition is this ownership-constrained uncertainty set and its consequences for experimental inference.

## Close antecedents define a narrow original connection

**Hall and Fienberg (2012) is the most important additional predecessor.** They construct confidence sets of bipartite matchings and optimize downstream statistics over them, including regression coefficients. They explicitly distinguish uncertainty about the oracle statistic from uncertainty about the population parameter. Their linkage permutation test is not a randomized-treatment Fisher test. The present work supplies the treatment-design reference law and the full-bijection reduction that turns an ownership set into a computable causal p-value. The broad principle of propagating matching sets into inference must be credited to this earlier work. ([Hall–Fienberg, Sections 2–5](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rjhall/linkage.pdf))

Heng and Shaw provide the closest causal sensitivity framework: optimize over binary outcome alterations capable of overturning a randomization-test decision. Their formulation permits additional linear restrictions. Introducing a matching matrix \(X\), imposing candidate and identity-budget restrictions, and requiring corrected outcomes \(v=Xy\) connects our procedure directly to theirs. Their main implemented sharp-null procedure uses a chi-square approximation; their broader framework includes other designs and tests. They do not supply the current candidate-identity model or common-reference-law calculation. This is a **focused extension of the uncertainty description and its implementation**, not an unrelated optimization paradigm. ([Heng–Shaw, Definition 1 and Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.03111))

Ota and Imaizumi (2026) also propagate candidate permutations into valid regression inference, under a fixed-design Gaussian model. Thus “inference without uniquely recovering identities” is already occupied. Their probability model differs from finite-population treatment randomization. Maximizing a p-value over a nuisance confidence set and adding its containment-failure bound follows Berger–Boos. These antecedents establish the ingredients without, in the inspected texts, establishing the exact current causal procedure. **A short derivation can still support an original specialization; its brevity limits claims about theoretical invention, not whether the resulting procedure contributes.** ([Ota–Imaizumi, Theorems 4.1–4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14872v2); [Berger–Boos](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476836))

## The structural results explain the method

The swap identity—weight difference times outcome difference—explains which ambiguities matter. However, Wortman and Reiter already show that exchanging treated outcomes within a propensity subclass preserves an unadjusted contrast, while regression adjustment can change it. Turkcapar and Krishnan already optimize weighted query results over candidate matchings, and query-driven entity resolution explicitly identifies unresolved identity edges that cannot change a query answer. **Neither harmless linkage errors nor analysis-specific identity resolution is a new principle.** ([Wortman–Reiter, Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.03631); [Turkcapar–Krishnan, Sections 3.1–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05178v1); [Altwaijry et al., Section 5.4](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol6/p1846-altwaijry.pdf))

The cycle characterization is classical assignment structure specialized to costs \(w_i y_j\). Constant assignment objectives, alternating-cycle decompositions, component separability, and ordering arguments underlie the zero-width criterion and complete-block formula. They should be presented as attributed tools that deliver a causal diagnostic. Their useful output is determining whether unresolved identities leave the **realized oracle statistic** unchanged and which components contribute uncertainty. This is not identification of the average treatment effect from one assignment, nor automatic invariance of standard errors. ([Ćustić–Klinz, constant-objective assignment characterization](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/4364.pdf); [Roughgarden, alternating-cycle criterion](https://theory.stanford.edu/~tim/w16/l/l5.pdf))

The controlled example isolates the diagnostic's meaning. With the same accepted linkage, 75% identity accuracy, two candidates per record, and five budget-feasible reconstructions, two graphs produce robust p-values **2/70 versus 34/70**. The first permits only harmless within-arm ambiguity; the second permits consequential exchanges. This is a clean demonstration beyond scalar summaries, not independent proof of a new statistical principle. Across all assignments in the accompanying experiment, graph restrictions help but leave large power losses: at effects 2 and 4, oracle rejection is 100%, graph rejection is 6/70, and accuracy-only rejection is zero at 5%. ([Example and exhaustive results](../research/graph-information.md))

## Validity has clear boundaries and existing precedents

The current result assumes known complete randomization, full overlap, a bijection, and a feasible set containing the true linkage. It tests **Fisher's sharp no-effect null**, not the weak null of zero average effect. If whole-set containment can fail with probability at most \(\delta\), adding \(\delta\) to the maximized p-value supplies the standard correction. The implementation accepts that bound; it does not estimate it. Pairwise candidate recall does not establish simultaneous containment. ([Current scope and proof](../research/graph-information.md))

The earlier audit also needs correction on the research frontier: **whole-matching calibration is not untouched territory**. Hall–Fienberg propose model-based matching-set coverage, and Ota–Imaizumi develop permutation-set inference under their regression model. A different validation-based construction could contribute, but requires comparison with these results. Solving that separate problem is not necessary to report the present procedure honestly as a sensitivity analysis conditional on specified restrictions. ([Hall–Fienberg, equation (7)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rjhall/linkage.pdf); [Ota–Imaizumi](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14872v2))

## One realistic experiment would test practical value

The decisive next step is one randomized dataset with known identities and genuinely ambiguous identifier-derived candidates. Keep the accepted matching, donor pool, and identity-error information identical across analyses; vary only whether candidate restrictions are retained. Measure exact-test validity, improvements in robust conclusions, power losses, and computation. Separately examine candidate omissions and budget misspecification rather than silently forcing truth into every graph. This tests whether the proposed information survives a realistic linkage process. The current Rajasthan analysis cannot yet validate this test because its historical assignment support and reconstruction containment remain unverified. ([Current application boundary](../research/graph-information.md))

There is no need to invent a new optimizer or solve arbitrary covariate error before doing that comparison. If realistic graphs materially change conclusions at credible restrictions, the project has demonstrated an applied methodological contribution. If useful results occur only in engineered examples, a tutorial or diagnostic note is the better form. The distinction should turn on evidence of use, not the size of the theorem.

## Conclusion

A defensible claim is: **“We develop a randomization sensitivity analysis for uncertain record identities that retains candidate-link restrictions and one-to-one donor conservation. Under complete randomization and full overlap, we compute an exact worst-case sharp-null p-value and identify which unresolved identities can change the causal conclusion.”** The literature search supports this focused connection as an original contribution worth evaluating; it does not certify an absolute first-publication claim.

Continue the narrow note and its realistic test. The contribution should survive removing the slogan “accuracy is insufficient”: what remains is a specified uncertainty object, a usable causal test, a validity argument, and an inspectable worst-case linkage. Those are already present. Evidence that they improve a realistic analysis is the remaining substantive question.
