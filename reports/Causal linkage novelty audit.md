# Existing results narrow causal linkage novelty

**The implemented compendium does not currently support a strong original statistical-method claim. Stop developing its present collection of results as a methods paper.** This judgment follows comparisons of the actual formulas, assumptions, and probability experiments, not merely overlap in subject matter. The strongest surviving distinction is an exact finite-population expectation for arbitrary fixed one-to-one errors and heterogeneous treatment effects; its exact statement was not located, but it is a short extension of established linkage algebra and randomization moments. The validation estimator is standard difference estimation, its variance result composes standard sampling identities, and the graph procedures combine established assignment optimization with nuisance-parameter projection. Candidate graphs nevertheless contain real causal information beyond precision or an error budget. That insight warrants a specific further question, not a claim that the current implementation has answered it.

Audit date: 8 October 2026. This report evaluates the current manuscript and implementation against primary-source research recorded in three accompanying research notes. “No” below means insufficient support for a substantive methods contribution, not proof that every displayed equation has appeared verbatim elsewhere.

**The subtle bias result changes the experiment, but its main lesson is established.**

Let \(F_i\) indicate that a fixed outcome permutation leaves unit \(i\) correctly linked, \(q=N^{-1}\sum_iF_i\), and \(\tau_i\) be its treatment effect. Under complete randomization, the manuscript proves

\[
E_Z(\widehat\tau_L)=\frac{Nq-1}{N-1}\tau_N+
\frac{N}{N-1}\operatorname{Cov}_N(F_i,\tau_i).
\]

The finite factor is already implicit in Chambers's explicitly displayed exchangeable linkage matrix. His equations (1)–(6), printed pp. 9–10, give \(E(A\mid X)=(\lambda-\gamma)I+\gamma\mathbf1\mathbf1'\), with \(\gamma=(1-\lambda)/(N-1)\); equation (11), p. 12, derives naive regression bias. For any fixed zero-sum contrast \(w\), our algebraic reduction is

\[
E_A(w'Ay\mid y,w)=\frac{N\lambda-1}{N-1}w'y.
\]

Thus the finite correction to simple attenuation is established linkage algebra. Chambers attributes the exchangeable model to Neter, Maynes, and Ramanathan (1965). ([Chambers, 2009, primary report](https://statsnz.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p20045coll4/id/288/download))

**These are not identical theorems.** Chambers averages random linkage holding the analysis data fixed. Our proposition averages random assignment holding an arbitrary permutation fixed; that permutation need not satisfy exchangeable off-diagonal probabilities. It also allows correct links to concentrate among units with unusual effects. Its short proof, however, uses only \(E_Z(w_iZ_j)=1/N\) when \(i=j\), and \(-1/[N(N-1)]\) otherwise, plus the permutation's unit column sums. Detailed permutation cycles disappear from the expectation. This is a useful design-based clarification, but it provides no new feasible correction when the correctness–effect covariance is unknown.

The covariance interpretation has a particularly close antecedent previously missing from the compendium. **Tahamont et al.'s Appendix E is explicitly about treatment heterogeneity correlated with matching error.** It derives a subgroup-weighted effect \(\sum_gP(g)(\mathrm{TPR}_g-\mathrm{FPR}_g)\tau_g\), allowing amplification when reliability and opposing subgroup effects align. Writing \(r_g=\mathrm{TPR}_g-\mathrm{FPR}_g\), this equals \(E(r)E(\tau)+\operatorname{Cov}(r,\tau)\). Their binary outcome-ascertainment mechanism differs from a permutation, but the substantive lesson that average linkage quality does not determine causal bias is explicit. ([Tahamont et al., author preproof, Appendix E, appendix p. 7](https://iamshiyan.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/tahamont2020_preproof.pdf))

Other seemingly subtle results have still closer antecedents. Wortman and Reiter §3.1 explicitly show that swapping two treated units' outcomes within a propensity subclass leaves the difference in means unchanged, then warn that regression adjustment need not remain unchanged. Their §3.2 discusses treatment-crossing errors and sign reversal. Our observational example maps exactly into Nab et al.'s misclassified binary-confounder model: confounder prevalence \(1/2\), treatment probabilities \(.2,.8\), outcome \(Y=Z+X\), and sensitivity and specificity \((1+q)/2\). The resulting bias \(.6(1-q^2)/(1-.36q^2)\) is a parameter specialization, even when the propensity model conditional on the measured covariate is correct. ([Wortman–Reiter, §§3.1–3.2, pp. 6–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.03631); [Nab et al., Figure 1B and equation (4)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7523582/))

**Validation buys a second probability design, using established estimators.**

The manuscript fixes all linked scores \(h_i\) before independently sampling \(k\) anchors for error-free validation. Its estimator is

\[
\widehat\tau_V=H+\frac Nk\sum_{i\in A}\{w_iY_i-h_i\},\qquad H=\sum_i h_i.
\]

Condition on the assignment, files, and scores. Substitute \(x_i=h_i\), \(y_i=w_iY_i\), and \(n=k\) into the survey difference estimator \(t_x+N(\bar y_s-\bar x_s)\): the expressions are identical. Its conditional unbiasedness and variance \(N^2(1-k/N)S_d^2/k\) are standard. Wrong treatment labels, outcome-dependent linkage, and dependence between competing links simply change the fixed auxiliary scores. ([Theory of Sample Surveys with R, §9.3, pp. 152–154, equations (9.41)–(9.43)](https://elibrary.narr.digital/xibrary/9783838543284))

The additional variance argument is also a direct composition. Conditional mean-zero validation error yields

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\tau_V)=\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\tau_O)+
E\{N^2(1-k/N)S_d^2/k\}.
\]

The residual sample variance estimates the second term. Express the full-data Neyman estimator as within-arm squared pair differences, and divide each validated pair contribution by its sampling probability \(k(k-1)/[N(N-1)]\). Its expectation recovers the full-data Neyman estimator, whose usual excess over oracle variance is \(S_\tau^2/N\). We did not locate this exact combined formula verbatim. Its derivation nonetheless requires no new linkage-specific inference principle. Conservative variance expectation also does not establish exact normal-interval coverage.

The closest contemporary comparator is especially consequential: **Barnatchez et al. (2025) explicitly handle simultaneous error-prone treatment and outcome measurements**, nonuniform validation, and differential measurement errors. Their Proposition 1 gives the familiar validated-residual augmentation, and §4 develops asymptotic inference. They assume iid observations, complete-data causal identification, validation missingness at random given observed data, positivity, and nuisance convergence conditions. Those assumptions do not automatically cover arbitrary globally coupled one-to-one errors. Conversely, our finite-population/SRS result does not discover joint-error correction; it supplies a simple design-based specialization. ([Barnatchez et al., §§2–4, Proposition 1, equations (7) and (9)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21777v1))

This distinction matters operationally. Fresh probability validation permits linked scores to depend on assignment and outcomes because unbiasedness comes from the second sampling design. It must recover the true causal score even for sampled unlinked anchors. A correct/incorrect match indicator alone is insufficient. Comparisons against methods given only match-status labels must account for that unequal information budget. Earlier causal control-variate work already uses validation information without requiring error-prone estimators themselves to identify the causal effect. ([Yang–Ding, §3.2, equation (5), Proposition 1](https://shuyang.wordpress.ncsu.edu/files/2022/11/Yang-Ding-2020-JASA-Combining-Multiple-Observational-Data-Sources-to-Estimate-Causal-Effects.pdf))

**Candidate constraints add information; the current optimization and coverage arguments are standard.**

For fixed assignment and arm sizes \(m,c\), set \(w_i=Z_i/m-(1-Z_i)/c\). Optimizing the reconstructed contrast is exactly a weighted assignment problem with edge weight \(w_i y_j\). Fixed-design OLS coefficients are analogous linear queries. Turkcapar and Krishnan §§3.1–3.3 already optimize weighted aggregates over candidate matchings and explain donor competition. Their printed saturation arguments need sign/cardinality qualifications; the relevant antecedent is the assignment framework, not every literal inequality in their formulation. Outside options become private dummy donors, overlap becomes cardinality restrictions, and erroneous-trusted-link or missing-edge budgets become side constraints. The exact conjunction was not located, but adding these constraints does not establish a new optimization principle. ([Turkcapar–Krishnan, §§3.1–3.3, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05178v1))

A particularly close inferential comparator is **Heng and Shaw's sensitivity analysis for binary outcome misclassification**. Definition 1 asks how much outcome error could reverse a randomization-test decision. Section 4.1 formulates an integer optimization and explicitly permits additional prior-information constraints. For binary donor outcomes, introduce assignment variables \(x_{ij}\) and impose \(v_i=\sum_jx_{ij}y_j\); identity feasibility then restricts their possible corrected outcome vectors. Their efficient implementations use design-specific reductions and approximations, so this is a formulation-level connection rather than a claim that their algorithms solve every identity graph. ([Heng–Shaw, Definition 1 and §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.03111); [published JCGS article, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2025.2461222))

The graph restrictions are substantively valuable. Take \(Z=(1,1,0,0)\), donor outcomes \((0,1,0,1)\), and candidate neighborhoods confined to donors \(\{1,2\}\) for the treated anchors and \(\{3,4\}\) for controls. Every feasible full linkage gives contrast zero. A generic outcome-error budget of two permits \((1,1,0,0)\), whose contrast is one, despite preserving the global outcome total. Graph components and donor exclusivity exclude that pattern. This is our explanatory construction, not an attributed priority result. Identity error and outcome disagreement also differ: a wrong donor can have the same outcome.

Turning estimator ranges into causal uncertainty uses established projection logic. If an oracle procedure fails with probability at most \(\alpha\) and the reconstruction set omits the truth with probability at most \(\delta\), their union can fail with probability at most \(\alpha+\delta\). Maximizing a valid nuisance-indexed p-value over a confidence set and adding its failure probability is the Berger–Boos construction. Independence is unnecessary; valid oracle inference and simultaneous containment are essential. Pairwise score calibration does not automatically establish containment of the entire true linkage. The implementation assumes the relevant restrictions rather than learning that guarantee from validation. ([Berger–Boos, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476836))

Nor does the simplest exact-test case hide a compelling computational contribution. With full bijections, complete randomization, and the unstudentized absolute difference in means under the sharp null, every reconstruction has the same outcome multiset and hence the same randomization distribution. **Maximizing its p-value amounts to finding a feasible observed contrast closest to zero.** Endpoints alone can miss gaps in attainable contrasts, but minimizing absolute contrast has a standard mixed-integer formulation. Harder settings include changing donor subsets, blocked designs with changing within-block outcome multisets, or nonlinear estimators; the current implementation does not solve those problems.

**Retain the useful compendium; require a stronger result before restarting a methods paper.**

The review-priority example correctly shows that a record with zero local outcome range can constrain another record's donor. But query-directed identity review already appears in Jeffery, Franklin, and Halevy (2008), using value of perfect information. Our best/worst one-step interval reduction differs from expected utility without establishing a new optimal review policy. The exact minimax criterion was not located. ([Jeffery et al., author publication page](https://research.google/pubs/pay-as-you-go-user-feedback-for-dataspace-systems/))

The two-unit nonidentification proof also remains useful exposition. With one treated unit, populations having homogeneous potential outcomes \((0,1)\) versus \((1,0)\) produce the same unordered outcome pair but opposite effects. This extends immediately to balanced even-sized populations. Unequal arm counts distinguish those particular populations, so the example does not characterize a general identification frontier. Related unlinked-regression work establishes symmetry-induced sign ambiguity in a different observation model; it is a conceptual antecedent, not an exact causal theorem containing our construction. ([Balabdaoui–Slawski–Steffani, §4.1, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14986v1))

The broader literature also defeats a high-precision-only baseline. Slawski covers linked treatment, covariates, and outcomes with explicit bias and inference results. Shan–Thomas–Gutman propagate treated-roster identity uncertainty and examine informative-linkage sensitivity; Guha–Reiter–Mercatanti use outcome-informed partial one-to-one Bayesian linkage. Their assumptions differ, and none is universal arbitrary-error robustness. ([Slawski, §§2.1–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14492v1); [Shan et al., §§2.3 and 5.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9222523/); [Guha et al., §2, equation (5)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09119))

A defensible description is: **“We specialize established methods to uncertain causal linkage, derive a finite-population permutation lemma, and provide a reproducible empirical audit.”** The code, proofs, simulations, and application can have practical value without methodological priority. A specific unproved research problem remains: obtain useful, scalable causal inference that exploits identity conservation while accounting for uncertain whole-graph recall, especially when reconstruction changes treatment, covariates, or the assignment-relevant outcome multiset. Any renewed investment should first compare constrained Heng–Shaw-style sensitivity against a proposed specialized method under the same validation information. If adding standard assignment constraints already performs adequately, the methodological case becomes weaker. This is a research question, not a promise of novelty.
